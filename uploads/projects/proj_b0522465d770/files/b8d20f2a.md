# Stakeholder Profile Context

The simulation should contain heterogeneous stakeholder roles rather than a single homogeneous community.

## 1. Public Officials

Primary interests:
- Policy implementation.
- Public service delivery.
- Budget management.
- Public communication.
- Measurable pilot outcomes.

Potential positions:
- Supportive, conditional, or concerned depending on fiscal and operational evidence.

## 2. Transit Riders

Primary interests:
- Affordable mobility.
- Reliability.
- Frequency.
- Accessibility.
- Travel convenience.

Potential positions:
- Often interested in the immediate passenger benefits but may raise concerns about overcrowding or reliability.

## 3. Local Businesses

Primary interests:
- Customer foot traffic.
- Weekend sales.
- Accessibility of commercial areas.
- Delivery and congestion conditions.

Potential positions:
- May support the program if increased mobility benefits commerce, while remaining concerned about implementation costs or crowding.

## 4. Taxpayer and Fiscal Groups

Primary interests:
- Municipal financial sustainability.
- Tax burden.
- Efficient public spending.
- Transparency of funding.

Potential positions:
- May question the program if funding is unclear or costs increase.

## 5. Environmental Advocates

Primary interests:
- Reduced vehicle use.
- Public transportation.
- Emissions reduction.
- Sustainable urban mobility.

Potential positions:
- May support the program while requesting evidence that it actually replaces private vehicle trips.

## 6. General Residents

Primary interests:
- Household costs.
- Mobility.
- Quality of public services.
- Safety.
- Fairness.

Potential positions:
- May vary according to transportation needs, neighborhood access, and perceived fiscal effects.

## Agent Diversity Requirement

Agents should not all agree with the same policy position.

Each simulated stakeholder should have distinct:
- Role.
- Goals.
- Concerns.
- Evidence preferences.
- Initial policy stance.
- Communication style.
- Interaction tendencies.

The simulation should allow positions to change as agents encounter arguments and information from other stakeholders.
