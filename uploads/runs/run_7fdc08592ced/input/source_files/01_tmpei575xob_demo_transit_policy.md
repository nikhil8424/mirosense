# Metro City Fare-Free Weekend Bus Program

## Policy Overview

Metro City is considering a six-month pilot program that makes all municipal bus services free to passengers on Saturdays and Sundays.

The proposed policy is intended to improve equitable access to public transportation, reduce weekend traffic congestion, increase commercial activity in downtown areas, and support environmental sustainability.

## Policy Objectives

1. Improve access to affordable public transportation.
2. Encourage residents to use buses instead of private vehicles.
3. Reduce weekend congestion and vehicle emissions.
4. Increase foot traffic for local businesses.
5. Evaluate whether fare-free weekend service should be continued or expanded.

## Key Stakeholders

### Public Officials
The Mayor and Transit Authority support the pilot because they expect improved mobility and environmental benefits.

### City Council and Environmental Advocates
Supporters emphasize equity, reduced vehicle use, and environmental benefits. Some may argue that the pilot provides useful evidence for future transit policy.

### Local Business Coalition
Local businesses may support the program if increased transit usage leads to higher weekend foot traffic and retail activity. They may also be concerned about overcrowding and operational reliability.

### Taxpayer and Fiscal Groups
Fiscal watchdogs may question the cost of eliminating fares. They may argue that the municipality needs a clearly identified funding source and may oppose expansion if the pilot increases the budget deficit.

### Citizens and Daily Commuters
Residents may value free transportation because it reduces travel costs. Some commuters may also raise concerns about overcrowding, reliability, safety, and whether resources would be better spent improving service frequency.

## Fiscal Context

The policy discussion includes concern about a potential municipal budget shortfall of approximately $4.2 million. Stakeholders may disagree about whether fare-free weekend service can be funded sustainably.

Possible funding mechanisms discussed by stakeholders include municipal budget reallocations, commercial parking fees, state or regional transportation grants, environmental grants, and other dedicated transit funding.

## Environmental Context

Supporters argue that free weekend transit could encourage residents to leave private vehicles at home. The expected environmental effect depends on whether additional bus trips replace private-vehicle trips or primarily generate new transit trips.

## Community Decision Problem

The central question is whether Metro City should implement the six-month fare-free weekend bus pilot and, if implemented, whether the program should later be continued or expanded.
